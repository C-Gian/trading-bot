## Evidence interpretation

Phase A exposed two different failure modes:

- permissive configurations generated meaningful support but materially adverse economics and
  triggered/faced the frozen risk screens;
- full corroboration configurations became extremely sparse, with positive point estimates based on
  only a few trades and therefore no admissible claim.

The evidence does not identify whether economic weakness or representation/architecture explains
most of the failure. Both may coexist.

The critical allocation question is whether Phase A identified a specific independently defensible
correction. It did not.

## Why no G2 is allocated

The following are not currently admitted as successor justifications:

- replacing hard conjunctions with weighted scores;
- using a different state-machine architecture;
- routing entries through the continuous forecast;
- trading more broadly from market state;
- replacing or altering P2;
- changing exits/reward multiples;
- fitted component weights/model ensembles.

All are plausible ideas, but after exposed G1 results they would consume new adaptive search without
independent evidence that they solve the observed economic problem.

## Reopening boundary

work, or an attractive weighting scheme is not a reopening trigger.

A future Astra review may consider only a narrowly documented material development such as:

- an independently substantiated architectural contradiction with a specific correction; or
- relevant external evidence for a fully specified materially distinct system inside Owner Mission
  V2.

Any proposal must state lineage, exposed information, expected practical value, finite budget and why
existing failures do not already answer it.

A material Owner mission/resource change may also justify reconsideration.
